LaptopScreenGuard Face Only

1. Install Python 3.12 x64 on Windows 10/11 x64.
2. Extract All. Exit any older LaptopScreenGuard instance before testing.
   If the old version starts on sign-in, disable its Startup entry to
   avoid two apps competing for the webcam (Win+R, shell:startup).
3. Run Install-LaptopScreenGuard.cmd under your Windows account while online. This installs
   OpenCV and Qt, downloads both face models and creates a sign-in entry.
4. Right-click the LaptopScreenGuard tray icon and choose Enroll My Face.
   Set and confirm an app PIN of at least six digits. Sit alone in good
   light and face the webcam. Wait for Owner face saved.
5. Step away or have someone else take your seat. The screen blacks out
   after two seconds without a match. Return alone to restore the screen.
   Keyboard and mouse resume when the overlay clears.
6. Use Replace Enrolled Face with your existing app PIN to change the face.
   Ctrl+Shift+F12 pauses/resumes. Exit the app before running Uninstall-LaptopScreenGuard.cmd.

Only face matching controls the overlay. A closed shutter, blocked lens,
or interrupted camera can still make your face unavailable, which triggers
the same two-second rule. A phone has no dedicated trigger.
The app stores a local face embedding and salted PIN hash, without saving
webcam photos. Photo/video spoofing is possible. Use Win+L for Windows lock.
Log: %USERPROFILE%\LaptopScreenGuard-Face-Only\LaptopScreenGuard.log
